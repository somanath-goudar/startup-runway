Frequently Asked Questions
Startup runway = current cash balance ÷ net monthly burn. If you have $500K and burn $50K/month net, you have 10 months. When MRR grows, net burn decreases each month — so proper runway simulates month by month: cash(n) = cash(n-1) − burn + mrr(n), where MRR compounds at your growth rate.
18–24 months is considered healthy. It gives you enough time to raise your next round (6–9 months process) with buffer remaining. Less than 12 months is the danger zone — start cutting costs or fundraising immediately. Less than 6 months requires emergency action.
Net burn = gross monthly expenses − monthly revenue (MRR). If you spend $80K/month and earn $30K MRR, your net burn is $50K/month. This is what determines your actual runway. Gross burn ignores revenue and overstates how fast you're depleting cash.
Funding needed = (target months × net burn) − current cash. E.g. for 18 months at $50K net burn with $300K cash: 18 × $50K − $300K = $600K. Always add 20% buffer for delays. Our reverse calculator above handles this automatically.
Start when you have 9–12 months of runway left. The typical VC process takes 6–9 months from first pitch to cash in bank. Starting with more runway gives you leverage — you're not raising from desperation. The "Fundraise By" date in your results above marks this threshold.
